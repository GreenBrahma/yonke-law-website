## Executive Summary — Headline Differences Between Missouri and Kansas

Missouri and Kansas share a border and a large share of the Kansas City–metro practice but diverge sharply on the doctrines that decide personal-injury cases. The most consequential cross-border differences are summarized below, with each point anchored to an official source cited in detail later in this catalog.

| Issue | Missouri | Kansas |
|---|---|---|
| General PI statute of limitations | **5 years** ([RSMo § 516.120](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=516.120)) | **2 years** ([K.S.A. 60-513(a)(4)](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch60/060_005_0013.html)) |
| Comparative fault | **Pure** comparative fault — plaintiff recovers even at 99% fault; products-liability rule codified at [RSMo § 537.765](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.765); general-negligence rule is **common law** under *Gustafson v. | Government tort caps | Sovereign immunity restored 1977 with limited waivers; cap **$300,000/person, $2,000,000/occurrence**, inflation-indexed ([RSMo §§ 537.600](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.600), [537.610](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.610)) | KTCA cap **$500,000/occurrence**, no punitives ([K.S.A. 75-6105](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch75/075_061_0005.html)) |

These distinctions repeatedly drive whether a case is filed in Missouri or Kansas, where venue is most favorable, and which damages theory will survive a motion in limine or directed verdict.

## Source Methodology and Veracity Protocol

The catalog uses a five-tier source priority and a four-grade veracity scheme.

**Source priority (highest first):**

1. Primary statutory text from the official state revisor (revisor.mo.gov for Missouri; ksrevisor.gov for Kansas).
2. Official court materials: published opinions of the Missouri and Kansas Supreme Courts and Courts of Appeals on kscourts.gov, courts.mo.gov, and state-reporter equivalents.
3. Official court rules: Missouri Supreme Court Rules published through courts.mo.gov and the Missouri Bar (news.mobar.org for adoption orders); Kansas Supreme Court Rules at kscourts.gov.
4. Official agency sources: Missouri Department of Labor (labor.mo.gov); Kansas Department of Labor (dol.ks.gov); state insurance, healing-arts, and motor-carrier boards.
5. Official pattern jury instructions: Missouri Approved Instructions (MAI), published only by the Missouri Bar under Supreme Court order, and Kansas PIK Civil 4th, published by the Kansas Judicial Council ([kjc.ks.gov](https://www.kjc.ks.gov/publications/pik-civil-4th)).

**Veracity grades used in this catalog:**

- **A — Verified official statute / rule** — primary statutory or rule text confirmed at an official .gov or .gov-equivalent source and read for the proposition cited.
- **B — Official court / common law** — supported by a published state-court opinion accessed through an official court source (or through a citator confirming the holding is current).
- **C — Agency source** — supported by an official state agency publication.
- **D — Unresolved / case-law check needed** — primary text not retrieved in this session, source is a secondary summary, or constitutional/currency status is actively contested. Counsel must verify on Westlaw/Lexis or via direct court access before relying.

**No firm blogs, no Nolo, no aggregator content** is treated as authority. Where an official source is unavailable for a proposition, the catalog says so explicitly and flags the gap.

## 1. Foundational Negligence and Comparative Fault

**Prompt:** "What are the elements of a negligence personal-injury claim in Missouri and Kansas, and how does each state apportion fault when the plaintiff is partly responsible?"

**Verified Answer.** Both states require the four classic negligence elements: duty, breach, causation, and damages. The states diverge sharply on apportionment.

- **Missouri — pure comparative fault.** A plaintiff's recovery is reduced by the plaintiff's percentage of fault but never barred, even if the plaintiff is 99% at fault. For **products liability**, the rule is codified at [RSMo § 537.765](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.765). For **general negligence**, the pure rule is **common law**, adopted by the Missouri Supreme Court in *Gustafson v. Benda*, 661 S.W.2d 11 (Mo. banc 1983). It is not codified in a single negligence statute, a frequent point of confusion. Joint-and-several liability is then governed by [RSMo § 537.067](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.067): a defendant 51%-or-more at fault is jointly and severally liable; one below 51% is severally liable only.
- **Kansas — modified comparative fault with a 50% bar.** A plaintiff who is **50% or more** at fault recovers nothing; below 50%, damages are reduced proportionally. Kansas abolished joint-and-several liability — each defendant pays only its own share. The statute requires a special verdict apportioning percentages. Authority: [K.S.A. 60-258a](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch60/060_005_0258a.html). The four-element negligence framework and ordinary-care standard are reflected in [PIK Civil 4th 106.01](https://www.kjc.ks.gov/publications/pik-civil-4th).

**Official Resource Links:**

- Missouri: [RSMo § 537.765](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.765); [RSMo § 537.067](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.067); *Gustafson v. Benda* (Westlaw/Lexis).
- Kansas: [K.S.A. 60-258a](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch60/060_005_0258a.html); [PIK Civil 4th — Kansas Judicial Council](https://www.kjc.ks.gov/publications/pik-civil-4th).

**Veracity Check:** A for the Missouri products-liability and joint-and-several statutes, the Kansas comparative-fault statute, and the PIK reference. B (official common law) for Missouri general-negligence pure comparative fault — *Gustafson* is binding Missouri Supreme Court precedent but not a statute; confirm current validity via citator.

## 4. Auto Crashes (Liability, Coverage Minimums, No-Fault)

**Prompt:** "What is the framework for auto-collision personal-injury claims in Missouri and Kansas, including minimum coverages and whether PIP applies?"

**Verified Answer.**

- **Missouri.** Pure tort/at-fault state with no PIP mandate. Minimum auto liability: **$25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident / $25,000 property damage**, effective July 1, 2019 — [RSMo § 303.030](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=303.030). Every policy must include UM coverage at those minimum BI limits, and UIM is treated as included in UM by case law (*Cook v. Pedigo*) under [RSMo § 379.203](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=379.203). Stacking is generally allowed unless excluded by policy; fleet stacking is barred.
- **Kansas.** **Modified no-fault** under the Kansas Automobile Injury Reparations Act (KAIRA), [K.S.A. 40-3101 et seq.](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0001.html). Mandatory PIP: $4,500 medical, $4,500 rehab, $900/month income (1 year), $25/day household services, $2,000 funeral. To sue in tort for pain and suffering, the plaintiff must meet the **tort threshold** at [K.S.A. 40-3117](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0017.html): medical bills over $2,000, permanent injury or disfigurement, permanent loss of bodily function, fracture (certain types), loss of a body part, or death. Minimum auto liability 25/50/25 — [K.S.A. 40-3107](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0007.html). UM/UIM mandatory at liability limits unless rejected in writing — [K.S.A. 40-284](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_002_0084.html).

**Official Resource Links:**

- Missouri: [§ 303.030](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=303.030); [§ 379.203](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=379.203).
- Kansas: [K.S.A. ch. 40 art. 31](https://www.kslegislature.gov/laws/040_000_0000_chapter/040_031_0000_article/); [§ 40-284](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_002_0084.html).

**Veracity Check:** A. Verify current dollar minimums and PIP figures before quoting to clients (legislative updates occur periodically).

## 5. Trucking and Commercial-Motor-Vehicle Crashes

**Prompt:** "How are commercial motor-vehicle (CMV) and trucking cases handled in Missouri and Kansas, including federal regulation adoption and minimum insurance?"

**Verified Answer.**

- **Missouri** adopts the federal FMCSR (49 CFR Parts 390–397) for both interstate and intrastate CMVs at [RSMo § 307.400](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=307.400). Out-of-service orders are admissible as prima facie evidence under [RSMo § 226.009](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=226.009). Negligent-hiring, retention, supervision, and entrustment theories remain **common law**.
- **Kansas** motor carriers regulated by the Kansas Corporation Commission. Intrastate carriers must file proof of insurance: **$100,000/person, $300,000/accident BI, $50,000 PD** — [K.S.A. 66-1,128](https://www.ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch66/066_001_0128.html). Interstate carriers are subject to federal **FMCSA minimums (typically $750,000 CSL** general freight; $1M-$5M for hazmat/passengers) — see [49 CFR § 387.9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-387.9).

**Official Resource Links:** [FMCSA homepage](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov); [Missouri Highway Patrol Commercial Vehicle Enforcement](https://www.mshp.dps.missouri.gov); [Kansas Corporation Commission](https://www.kcc.ks.gov).

**Veracity Check:** A for the statutes. D for negligent-hiring/entrustment theories — common-law-based; verify via current Missouri and Kansas appellate citators.

## 6. Motorcycle Accidents and Seatbelt/Helmet Evidence

**Prompt:** "How do Missouri and Kansas treat motorcycle riding, helmet use, and seatbelt non-use as evidence of comparative fault?"

**Verified Answer.**

- **Missouri.** Operating a motorcycle, **standing alone, is not evidence of comparative negligence** in a motor-vehicle damages action — [RSMo § 537.055](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.055). Seatbelt non-use is **not** comparative-fault evidence; with expert proof of causation between non-use and injuries, damages may be reduced by **up to 1%** of the award (post-fault apportionment) — [RSMo § 307.178](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=307.178). The 1% cap does **not** apply in product-design/defect cases, where seatbelt non-use is admissible for comparative fault, causation, and mitigation. Missouri's adult motorcycle-helmet requirement was largely repealed in 2020 — verify current text of helmet statutes.
- **Kansas.** No statute parallels § 537.055 conclusively in this catalog; common law and PIK Civil instructions govern. Kansas requires helmets for riders **under 18** ([K.S.A. 8-1598](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch08/008_015_0098.html)). Admissibility of helmet/seatbelt non-use as comparative-fault evidence is **case-law-dependent** and should be verified.

**Veracity Check:** A for Missouri § 537.055 and § 307.178. D for Kansas helmet/seatbelt admissibility — verify via Kansas appellate decisions and PIK Civil notes.

## 7. Bicycle and Pedestrian Injuries

**Prompt:** "What rules of the road and statutory rights govern bicycle and pedestrian injury claims in Missouri and Kansas?"

**Verified Answer.** Both states treat bicyclists as having the rights and duties of vehicle operators on roadways and impose stop/yield duties on pedestrians outside crosswalks. The state vehicle codes govern.

- **Missouri:** Rules of the road generally at [RSMo ch. 304](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/PageSelect.aspx?chapter=304) (operation; pedestrian right-of-way at crosswalks; bicycles treated as vehicles). Comparative-fault apportionment per *Gustafson*.
- **Kansas:** Rules of the road at [K.S.A. ch. 8 art. 15](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch08/), including pedestrian and bicycle provisions. KAIRA PIP benefits apply to occupants and to pedestrians struck by a covered motor vehicle — [K.S.A. 40-3107(f)](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0007.html). Modified comparative fault and 50% bar apply.

**Veracity Check:** A for chapter-level citations. D for specific subsection texts on rights-of-way and bicycle-as-vehicle classification — pull the exact subsection before pleading.

## 8. UM/UIM and PIP / No-Fault Issues

**Prompt:** "How do uninsured/underinsured-motorist coverage and PIP benefits work in Missouri and Kansas, and what are the subrogation rules?"

**Verified Answer.**

- **Missouri UM/UIM.** Mandatory UM at minimum BI limits ([RSMo § 379.203](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=379.203)); UIM is recognized within the UM framework by case law (*Cook v. Pedigo*). UM follows the insured personally; hit-and-run covered without physical-contact requirement. Stacking permitted absent enforceable policy exclusion. **No PIP mandate** statewide.
- **Kansas PIP.** Mandatory PIP under [K.S.A. 40-3107](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0007.html). PIP subrogation/reimbursement is **only for duplicative damages** — [K.S.A. 40-3113](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0013.html), [40-3113a](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_031_0013a.html). The *Kroeker v. State Farm* doctrine bars PIP recoupment from a recovery that does not in fact duplicate PIP benefits already paid. If the injured party does not file a third-party action within **18 months**, the right transfers to the PIP insurer. UM/UIM mandatory at liability limits unless rejected in writing — [K.S.A. 40-284](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch40/040_002_0084.html); anti-stacking presumption.

**Veracity Check:** A for statutes. B for *Cook v. Pedigo* and *Kroeker* — confirm citators before relying.

## 9. Medical Malpractice

**Prompt:** "What are the procedural gatekeepers and damages rules for medical-malpractice cases in Missouri and Kansas?"

**Verified Answer.**

- **Missouri — affidavit of merit.** Plaintiff **must file an affidavit** within 90 days of the petition (extendable once 90 days for good cause) stating a legally qualified health-care provider in the same profession and specialty has opined that defendant failed to use reasonable care and that the failure caused damages. ## 12. Premises Liability / Slip and Fall

## 13. Negligent Security and Third-Party Criminal Acts

## 26. Common-Carrier and Aviation/Rail Injuries (Cross-Reference)

**Prompt:** "What special rules apply to common-carrier and railroad/aviation passenger and worker injury claims in Missouri and Kansas?"

**Verified Answer.**

- **Missouri.** Railroad and certain mine-operator employees: the **fellow-servant rule is abolished**, [RSMo § 537.180](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=537.180). Most interstate railroad-worker claims are governed by federal **FELA** (45 U.S.C. § 51 et seq.). General common-carrier negligence — heightened duty of care — is common law in both states.
- **Kansas.** Common-carrier venue rule: any county through which the defendant regularly operates — [K.S.A. 60-606(a)](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch60/060_006_0006.html).

**Veracity Check:** A for statutes. B for the federal FELA reference.

## Final Notes for Counsel

This catalog is designed as a working reference for AI-assisted prompt drafting and as a one-stop checklist of official sources. Counsel should treat every figure (caps, minimums, inflation-adjusted amounts) as **subject to current verification** before disclosure to a client or use in a pleading. The **Grade-D** entries above are the highest-risk for stale or contested authority — most prominently the Missouri med-mal noneconomic cap and the Missouri punitive cap, both re-enacted in 2020 and whose constitutional status has not been definitively re-resolved by the Missouri Supreme Court at the time of this draft. Counsel should run a fresh appellate-citator pass on those statutes before relying on the cap figures.

For pattern jury instructions, the **full text of MAI** is not freely available online and must be accessed through the [Missouri Bar publications](https://mobar.org). The **full text of PIK Civil 4th** is available on subscription through the [Kansas Judicial Council](https://www.kjc.ks.gov/publications/pik-civil-4th).

For Missouri civil-procedure rule text, **courts.mo.gov** is the authoritative source and must be accessed via a human browser (the site restricts automated access).

End of catalog.
